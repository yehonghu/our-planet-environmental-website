Water / pollution signal

01 / Ocean debris

What reaches
the water stays
in the system.

Plastic moves from streets and shorelines through rivers and currents. The scale is planetary, but the most useful responses begin with preventing waste before it reaches a coast.

2,000garbage-truck equivalents of plastic enter oceans, rivers, and lakes each day.
8M+tons of plastic are estimated to enter the ocean each year.

Air / climate signal

02 / Climate evidence

The atmosphere
keeps a record.

Evidence for a warming climate can be read across the atmosphere, oceans, land, and ice. Long-running observations turn a changing landscape into a scientific record of a system under stress.

1°Crise in average surface temperature since the late nineteenth century.

Life / habitat signal

03 / Biodiversity

Life is not
a backdrop.

Forests, coasts, grasslands, and freshwater systems do more than look alive. They hold water, regulate climate, feed communities, and support a web of species that cannot be replaced once lost.

1Manimal and plant species are threatened with extinction, many within decades.
20%decline in average native-species abundance across many land habitats since 1900.

04 / Environmental justice

The burden is
not shared
equally.

Environmental change is also a question of access, safety, and representation. A credible response needs to account for the communities closest to climate impacts and include their knowledge in decisions about land, water, and resilience.

1/4of global land is traditionally owned, managed, used, or occupied by Indigenous Peoples.
35%of formally protected areas overlap with lands used or managed by Indigenous Peoples and local communities.
