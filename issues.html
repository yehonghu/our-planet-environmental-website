Plastic Pollution

Oceans drowning in plastic

The theme of World Environment Day is "Solutions to Plastic Pollution." The theme calls for global action to end plastic pollution. Every day, the equivalent of 2,000 garbage trucks full of plastic is dumped into the world's oceans, rivers and lakes.

Plastic pollution is a global problem that transcends national borders. The environmental, social, economic and health risks of plastics need to be assessed in conjunction with other environmental stressors such as climate change, ecosystem degradation and resource use.

Climate Change

A planet warming faster than ever

NASA is a global leader in climate research and Earth science. While NASA's role does not include setting climate policy or prescribing particular responses or solutions to climate change, its responsibilities do include providing the robust scientific data needed to understand climate change.

The scientific consensus is clear: human activities — primarily the burning of fossil fuels — are driving unprecedented changes to Earth's climate system, with consequences that will last for centuries.

1.5°C critical warming threshold to stay below
3.6mm average annual sea level rise

Global Cooperation

Canada's role in global environmental protection

Canada is working to advance global environmental protection. Canada's international assistance focuses on countries with a high impact on climate change. Canada is committed to supporting developing countries in their transition to a low-carbon, climate-resilient world.

Through Global Affairs Canada, the country channels significant climate finance to support adaptation and mitigation efforts in vulnerable nations, recognizing that climate change is a shared global challenge requiring coordinated international response.

Gender & Environment

Climate change is not gender-neutral

Climate change and environmental degradation disproportionately affect women and girls, who are more restricted in their access to productive resources, the gendered division of labour — including greater responsibility for accessing water, fuel and food — and face gender gaps in employment and education.

Addressing these intersecting inequalities requires a gender-responsive approach to environmental policy that recognizes and addresses the structural barriers women face in adapting to climate impacts.

80% of climate displaced people are women
3x more likely to die in climate disasters

Biodiversity & UNEP

The interconnected web of life

The United Nations Environment Programme (UNEP) has shown that the problem of plastic pollution does not exist in isolation. The environmental, social, economic and health risks of plastics need to be assessed in conjunction with other environmental stressors such as climate change, ecosystem degradation and resource use.

Biodiversity loss and climate change are deeply intertwined crises. Healthy ecosystems are our greatest natural allies in fighting climate change — they absorb carbon, regulate water cycles, and support the food systems that billions of people depend on.
